# Learning route patterns

## Choose a route

Select the route from the learner's required output, not from the topic label. The same topic can use different routes.

## Knowledge

Use when the result is understanding, explaining, comparing, or forming a mental model.

Design units around:

1. essential vocabulary and distinctions;
2. causal or structural relationships;
3. counterexamples and common confusions;
4. synthesis across concepts;
5. optional application to the learner's context.

Default evidence: free recall, explanation, concept comparison, error correction, and a synthesis map. Avoid turning the route into a reading list.

## Practical skill

Use when the learner must produce, operate, diagnose, or build something.

Design units around:

1. environment and safety prerequisites;
2. smallest executable technique;
3. guided practice with fading support;
4. representative tasks;
5. failure diagnosis;
6. an authentic final deliverable.

Default evidence: working artifacts, observed procedures, tests, quality checks, and independent troubleshooting. Require L3 for the core workflow.

## Professional research

Use when the result is a decision, recommendation, report, market view, strategy, or evidence-backed judgment.

Design units around:

1. decision question and scope;
2. terminology and market or domain structure;
3. source strategy and evidence hierarchy;
4. analytical framework;
5. conflicting evidence and uncertainty;
6. synthesis into a decision artifact;
7. challenge or red-team review.

Default evidence: cited research notes, source-quality decisions, defensible calculations, explicit inference, and a decision-ready deliverable. A defended report is normally L3 authentic application; use L4 only when the learner must adapt the research method to a materially different decision context. Do not reward information volume without synthesis.

## Exam or certification

Use when performance is defined by a published assessment blueprint or interview standard.

Design units around:

1. exam blueprint and weighting;
2. baseline diagnostic by domain;
3. prerequisite repair;
4. timed and untimed practice;
5. error log by cause;
6. cumulative mixed review;
7. realistic mock assessment;
8. final readiness decision.

Default evidence: fresh closed-book attempts, timed performance where relevant, error-pattern reduction, and mock results. Do not equate question-bank familiarity with readiness.

## Roadmap invariants

Every route must:

- state the final observable capability;
- show prerequisite dependencies;
- keep units small enough for one or a few sessions;
- assign a target level per unit;
- specify acceptable evidence before instruction begins;
- include review and transfer;
- adapt after evidence changes.

## Scope control

Use three buckets:

- `core`: required for the stated outcome;
- `supporting`: useful if time permits or a prerequisite gap appears;
- `later`: explicitly excluded from the current project.

When time is tight, reduce scope before weakening the evidence standard.
